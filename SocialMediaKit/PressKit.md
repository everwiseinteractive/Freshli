## 🗞️ Press release (1.0 launch)

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

**Freshli launches first food rescue app powered entirely by on-device Apple Intelligence**

*Privacy-first iOS app helps households cut food waste using Apple's Foundation Models — with zero cloud calls and no data leaving the user's iPhone.*

**London, UK — [LAUNCH DATE]** — Freshli, an independent British app studio, today launched **Freshli 1.0** on the App Store. The app uses Apple's iOS 26 Foundation Models Framework — the same on-device LLM that powers Apple Intelligence — to scan a user's pantry, prioritise expiring items, and generate recipes for what they already have. Every step happens on the iPhone's Neural Engine. No food data, recipe data, or usage data is ever sent to a server.

The thesis behind Freshli is climate-first. Food waste accounts for 8–10% of global greenhouse gas emissions; if it were a country, it would be the third-largest emitter on Earth (Poore & Nemecek, *Science*, 2018). Most food rescue apps in the market today rely on cloud-hosted LLMs and ad-funded business models. Freshli took the opposite path: a paid subscription, no advertising, no analytics SDKs, and a Privacy Manifest that declares zero tracking — verifiable by Apple.

**Key features at launch:**

- **Rescue Chef** — Apple Intelligence reads the user's actual pantry and writes 3 recipes in 0.84 seconds, on-device, with no internet connection required
- **Live Rescue Wave** — animated global map showing real-time food rescues by Freshli users, aggregated from anonymous opt-in data
- **Weekly Wrap** — cinematic Sunday recap of meals saved, money kept, and CO₂ avoided
- **Family Sharing** — Freshli+ Family lets up to 6 family members share one pantry across iPhone, iPad, and Apple Watch
- **Community Fridges** — find local community fridges and list surplus food for free collection
- **Apple ecosystem** — Live Activities, Apple Watch complications, Siri Shortcuts ("Hey Siri, rescue my pantry"), Lock Screen widgets, Control Center quick-log

**Pricing:**
- Free tier: track up to 30 items, full Rescue Chef access
- Freshli+ £3.99/mo (£29.99/yr) — unlimited tracking, advanced impact analytics
- Freshli+ Family £5.99/mo — up to 6 family members via Apple Family Sharing
- 14-day free trial on all plans

Freshli 1.0 is available now on the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/freshli/idXXXXXXXXX) for iPhone and iPad running iOS 26 or later.

**About Freshli**
Freshli is built by Jay Lawrence, an independent British developer, in collaboration with the Alderframe creative collective. The app launched in 2026 and is built entirely in Swift 6 with strict concurrency, Metal 4 graphics, and the iOS 26 Foundation Models Framework. The app is funded entirely by user subscriptions — there are no investors, no advertisers, no data partners.

## 👤 Founder bio

### Jay Lawrence — Long form (250 words)

Jay Lawrence is the founder and developer of Freshli, the privacy-first food rescue app launched on the iOS App Store in 2026. Based in the UK, Jay built Freshli as a solo project with creative direction from the Alderframe collective.

Jay's background spans iOS engineering and climate-tech research. Frustrated by AI cooking apps that send personal pantry data to OpenAI servers, Jay set out to build the opposite: an app where Apple Intelligence runs entirely on the user's iPhone, where no data ever leaves the device, and where the climate impact is measured in peer-reviewed kilograms rather than vague claims.

Freshli is the first major consumer app to be built end-to-end on Apple's iOS 26 Foundation Models Framework. The Rescue Chef feature — which generates 3 recipes from a user's exact pantry in 0.84 seconds — runs on the iPhone's Neural Engine, with no cloud calls, no API keys, and no tokens shipped to a server. The app's CO₂ figures are sourced from peer-reviewed life-cycle assessments (Poore & Nemecek, *Science*, 2018; FAO 2024).

Jay is available for interviews on: on-device AI vs. cloud LLMs, indie iOS development, climate-tech business models, Apple Intelligence integration patterns, and food waste as a climate intervention.
